## Claim

For factoid Q&A over a corpus updated in the last 24 hours, a graph-resident inference client outperforms a fine-tuned static model trained on yesterday's snapshot, purely due to recency — even at 10x latency penalty.

## Operationalization

Dataset: a corpus with a known "fact change" (e.g., contract amendment, regulatory update). Two snapshots: T-24h and T-now.
Metric: accuracy on 100 questions whose correct answer changed between snapshots.
Baseline: static model fine-tuned on T-24h snapshot.
Intervention: graph-resident client querying T-now corpus via ANN retrieval + LLM generation.
Acceptance criterion: graph-resident client achieves > 20% higher accuracy on delta questions, regardless of latency.

## Null Hypothesis

The static model's parametric knowledge is sufficient to answer delta questions correctly despite staleness, due to generalization from training context; graph recency provides no measurable accuracy gain.

## Experiment Spec

1. Take a corpus with a known update (e.g., a contract where clause 5 was amended).
2. Fine-tune or RAG-configure a static model on the T-24h snapshot.
3. Insert the updated blocks into the Nexum graph (T-now).
4. Build a 100-question eval set where correct answers depend on the updated blocks.
5. Evaluate both systems. Record accuracy and latency.
