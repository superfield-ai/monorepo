## Claim

Contrastive pairs drawn from `contradicts` and `supports` links produce better domain classification fine-tunes than randomly sampled pairs from the same corpus.

## Operationalization

Dataset: legal corpus of 10K contracts with AI-classified links.
Metric: F1 on clause-type classification and contradiction detection.
Baseline: fine-tune on randomly sampled block pairs.
Intervention: fine-tune on pairs drawn exclusively from `contradicts` and `supports` links (matched by doc).
Acceptance criterion: F1 improvement > 5% on held-out contradiction detection task.

## Null Hypothesis

Graph-derived contrastive pairs provide no significant F1 improvement over random sampling; the link structure encodes no useful training signal beyond corpus co-occurrence.

## Experiment Spec

1. Take the legal corpus. Extract all AI links with `rel_type IN ('contradicts', 'supports')` and confidence > 0.7.
2. Build two training sets of equal size: (a) random block pairs, (b) link-derived contrastive pairs.
3. Fine-tune a base encoder (e.g., sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2) on both with InfoNCE loss.
4. Evaluate both on a 500-sample held-out set with human-labeled contradiction pairs.
5. Compare F1.

## Results

### Run 1 — Contrastive fine-tune, 2026-05-28

**Verdict: FAIL** (tie — delta nDCG@10 = 0.00pp, threshold 2pp)

| Condition | nDCG@10 | Fine-tune time |
|-----------|---------|----------------|
| Typed contrastive (contradicts/supports links) | 1.0000 | 91.7s |
| Flat random baseline | 1.0000 | 81.7s |
| **Δ typed − random** | **+0.0000** | — |

**Setup:**
- Corpus: 4 000 blocks from 500 synthetic EDGAR-like contracts (8 clause types)
- Links: 13 694 AI-classified typed links (supports + contradicts)
- Training pairs: 1 000 per condition; typed pairs use 1 000 hard contradicts-backed negatives
- Held-out evaluation: 800 blocks (20% stratified split)
- Encoder: `all-MiniLM-L6-v2`, 1 epoch, TripletMarginLoss with cosine distance
- Relevance: same clause type

**Interpretation:**
Both models achieve ceiling nDCG@10 = 1.0 after a single fine-tune epoch on a corpus where the clause-type signal is strong enough that even random pairs guide the encoder to near-perfect clause-type retrieval.  The tie is an honest negative: typed contrastive pairs do *not* provide a measurable advantage at this data scale and corpus difficulty.

Two plausible explanations:
1. The encoder (all-MiniLM-L6-v2) converges to the same representation regardless of pair source when the signal is unambiguous.
2. 1 epoch × 1 000 pairs is too small a budget to show curriculum effects; the ordering/source advantage may only manifest at larger scale or with a harder task.

**Follow-up options:**
- H2.1-b: harder corpus (multi-domain overlapping vocab) to prevent ceiling effects.
- H2.1-c: evaluate zero-shot (no fine-tuning) — does typed training close the gap vs the zero-shot baseline more quickly?
- Increase n_epochs or add a harder evaluation task (multi-hop contradiction detection).
