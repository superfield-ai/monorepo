# Nexum Product Review — 2026-05-01

A critical read of `docs/product.md` against three questions:

1. Does this solve a real problem for companies and organizations?
2. Where are the technical risks?
3. What is the simplest, most impactful product — with no bloat or side quests?

The short version: there is a real product underneath this document, but it is buried under three layers of speculative architecture. The grooming, quorum, edge-embedding, and human-in-the-loop sampling sections are research projects, not shippable features. Strip them out and what remains is a credible "RAG with audit trails" platform that a regulated-industry customer might actually buy. Leave them in and Nexum is a five-year roadmap masquerading as a product spec.

---

## 1. Does it solve a real problem?

### Yes, for a specific buyer

The problem statement is genuinely real. Organizations do accumulate contradictory documents. Customer profiles do fragment across systems. Institutional knowledge does walk out the door when people leave. Anyone who has worked in a regulated industry — legal, healthcare, finance, public sector — knows that the *citation chain back to source* is not a nice-to-have; it is a procurement requirement.

The strongest pitch buried inside this document is **"RAG with auditable citations down to the paragraph."** That is a sentence a general counsel or a compliance officer will pay for. Block-level provenance, source-vs-synthesized typing, and the immutability/lineage guarantees on versioning together form a defensible answer to "where does this claim come from, and can you prove it?" Plain RAG cannot answer that question. Hebbia and Harvey can answer it inside their own walled gardens but not as a platform API.

### But the positioning is wobbly

Three problems with the framing:

**Platform positioning hides who buys.** "Direct customers are teams building document-intelligence products or internal tools" is the standard platform-API hedge. It is also the standard reason platform-API companies die: nobody is the buyer. The reference patterns (Handbook Agent, Strategic Planning Agent, Customer Profile Agent) implicitly assume the customer is going to build the agent *and* the end-user UX *and* the workflow integration. That is a 12-month project for a platform team that has not yet seen value. Most enterprises will not start that project on speculation.

**The three initial use cases are three different markets.** Software documentation, legal case files, and company handbooks share almost nothing operationally:
- Software docs has low willingness-to-pay and a developer-tool sales motion.
- Legal case files has very high willingness-to-pay but Harvey/Hebbia are entrenched and the regulatory/security bar (HIPAA-equivalents, court admissibility, privilege protection) is enormous.
- Handbook is a "we should have one of these" purchase that gets cut from every budget.

Picking three is picking none. The doc says "no single vertical is prioritized" — that is a positioning statement that loses every competitive bake-off.

**The future-LLM-architecture argument should be deleted.** The Flat File Ceiling section's strongest paragraph is the CLI-rebuttal one. The weakest is the speculation about "future reasoning machines [that] may operate natively on structured graphs." Betting product architecture on an unannounced shift in LLM I/O is fragile. The graph is justified by *today's* problems with consistency and provenance; no speculation needed. Keep the section, cut the futurism.

### What is actually missing from the problem framing

- **No customer-facing success metric.** What does a customer measure 90 days after deployment to decide whether to renew? The doc never says.
- **No business model.** Per-document pricing? Per-corpus? Per agent call? Per synthesis? This shapes the product more than any other decision and is absent.
- **No competitive map.** Glean, Hebbia, Harvey, Sana, Mendable, LlamaIndex Cloud, Vectara — at least three of these can be plausibly described in similar terms. What does Nexum specifically do that none of them do, and would a customer agree?
- **No discussion of who builds the agents.** The reference patterns assume a customer will build them. Will they? In what tool? On what stack? If the answer is "the customer figures it out," adoption stalls.

## 2. Technical risks

Twelve risks, ordered by how likely they are to kill the product.

### 2.1 Determinism claim is technically wrong

The Synthesis Quorum section says quorum agents are written so that "same source blocks plus same engine should produce the same output," and signing requires a content-hash match. **LLMs are not bitwise-deterministic.** Even at temperature 0, batch composition, KV-cache state, hardware non-determinism in floating-point reductions, and silent provider-side quantization changes all produce different token streams. The hash-match attestation will fail in production for nearly every non-trivial output.

Two consequences:
- The cryptographic-attestation framing is misleading. There is no meaningful signature here — just an LLM saying "this looks right."
- "Disagreement surfaces engine-level error" assumes engines that *can* agree. They cannot, on hashes.

If the quorum mechanism survives at all, it must be redefined as **semantic agreement**, evaluated by a judge agent, with a confidence threshold — not byte-level hash matching. That weakens the security/audit story considerably and reopens the question of whether quorum belongs in the product at all.

### 2.2 AI-inferred typed links are the hardest part of the system, and they're load-bearing

The differentiation from plain RAG is the typed link layer: contradicts, overrides, supports, elaborates, is-exception-to. These are **the hardest links to infer correctly** — they require multi-block reasoning that LLMs are notoriously inconsistent at. False contradictions and false overrides are not benign noise; they corrupt every downstream synthesis.

There is no discussion of:
- How link quality is evaluated.
- The cost of inferring links — naively O(N²) candidate pairs.
- How users discover that a `contradicts` link is hallucinated.
- The blast radius when a single false `overrides` link silently invalidates everything that depended on the older block.

This is the single technical area most likely to make the product feel unreliable, and the doc treats it as a one-paragraph capability.

### 2.3 Cost economics will not work as specified

Stacking the proposed mechanisms:
- Re-grounding pass = 1× synthesis cost
- Adversarial pairing = +1× critic cost
- Quorum (3-of-5 panel) = +5× synthesis cost
- Background grooming = continuous, multiplied by the above
- Human-in-the-loop selector = additional inference for question generation

A realistic upper-bound estimate is **6–10× the inference cost of a basic RAG pipeline**, running continuously. At current Claude/GPT rates, this is unaffordable for a software-docs customer and only marginally affordable for a legal customer paying enterprise prices. There is no unit-economics analysis in the doc. Without one, the architectural choices cannot be evaluated.

### 2.4 PDF ingestion is not a one-line feature

"POST a PDF and Nexum parses it into addressable blocks" describes the easiest case. Tables, footnotes, multi-column layouts, OCR'd scans, redacted documents, signed-and-stamped legal exhibits — these are the actual inputs in legal case files and finance. Existing players (Reducto, Unstructured, AWS Textract, Azure Document Intelligence) have spent years on this and still produce poor output on adversarial PDFs. The quality of every downstream link depends on the parser. This is an entire vendor-or-build decision that the doc treats as table stakes.

### 2.5 Postgres + pgvector will not scale to the implied corpus sizes

The implementation assumes a Postgres-backed graph (per CLAUDE.md context). For low millions of blocks this works. Beyond that:
- Vector index rebuilds become disruptive.
- Graph traversal queries (especially multi-hop) hit JOIN performance walls.
- Versioning with block-identity sharing requires fuzzy diff and reference-counting at every write.

Either commit to a sub-10M-block ceiling per corpus (and surface that limit honestly), or plan for a graph-DB or hybrid storage backend. The current doc implies the former while describing usage patterns that assume the latter.

### 2.6 Edge embeddings are unspecified and likely under-perform

"Both nodes and edges carry vector embeddings" is one sentence with large implications. What is embedded? The link's type label? The source-target text concatenation? The LLM's justification for asserting the link? Each choice has very different retrieval characteristics, and embedding-based retrieval over relationships is an open research area, not a shipped capability. Until there is a concrete spec and a benchmark, this should be cut from the product surface and revisited.

### 2.7 Polling cursor architecture creates a thundering herd

Every agent polls. The doc explicitly rejects push events. With multiple agents per corpus and multiple corpora per tenant, this becomes a thundering-herd problem at modest scale, and forces every customer to implement their own back-off logic. There is no discussion of cursor-cost limits, rate limits, long-poll support, or a server-side change-feed (e.g., Postgres `LISTEN/NOTIFY` exposed as a streaming endpoint). Pure polling is a defensible v0 choice but should be acknowledged as a temporary one.

### 2.8 Block-identity-across-versions is harder than described

"Unchanged blocks are shared across versions" requires a deterministic block-identity function that is robust to whitespace, reflow, OCR jitter, and parser updates. In practice this means content-based hashing with fuzzy matching and a similarity threshold — and once you have a threshold, you have edge cases (was a one-character correction the "same" block?). Get this wrong and link history silently breaks across versions. This single feature is probably 2–3 months of careful work.

### 2.9 Multi-tenancy, encryption, compliance are absent

For the named target verticals (legal, healthcare-adjacent), the doc says nothing about:
- Tenant isolation guarantees.
- Encryption at rest and in transit.
- Customer-managed keys.
- Audit log retention.
- SOC 2 / HIPAA / GDPR posture.
- Data residency.
- Right-to-be-forgotten in a graph that propagates blocks into derived synthesized blocks.

Any of these can be deal-breakers for the named buyers. The doc treats them as out-of-scope. They are not.

### 2.10 Synthesis-of-synthesis is forbidden in theory and inevitable in practice

The grooming section insists on re-grounding from source blocks. But useful intermediate syntheses — a customer profile, a case-fact-summary, a handbook section — are exactly the kind of artifact that another agent will reasonably want to *cite* rather than re-derive. The architecture needs an explicit notion of "synthesis depth" (how many hops from raw source) and rules about when synthesis-of-synthesis is allowed. Today the doc handles this only through prohibition, which won't survive contact with a real customer's workflow.

### 2.11 The grooming + adversarial + quorum + HITL stack is a research project

Each of these mechanisms individually is non-trivial. Stacked together — adversarial pairing on top of grooming on top of quorum on top of Bayesian sampling — they form an open research problem in multi-agent reasoning and ML safety. There is no published reference architecture that does all four well. Treating this as a product feature set commits the team to inventing what nobody has yet shipped.

### 2.12 The "model collapse" defense is necessary because the architecture creates the disease

Worth naming directly: the grooming section spends ~40 lines defending against model collapse. Model collapse is a problem *because the architecture invites continuous unsupervised synthesis-of-synthesis*. A simpler architecture — synthesis is triggered only by ingest events, never autonomously — does not need any of these defenses. The complexity is self-inflicted.

## 3. The simplest, most impactful product

Cut to the bone. Ship this:

### Core product (v0)

1. **Document ingestion** for PDF, DOCX, MD, plain text. Use a best-in-class third-party parser. Don't build it in-house.
2. **Block-level addressability** with stable IDs and embeddings.
3. **Naive document versioning** — full history, current-version-default, no block-identity sharing across versions yet. Adds 80% of the audit value at 20% of the engineering cost.
4. **Two link types only**:
   - Structural (citations resolved from text — deterministic, cheap)
   - Semantic (embedding similarity — deterministic, cheap)
5. **Query API**: semantic search, full-text search, single-hop graph traversal. No edge embeddings.
6. **Synthesis API**: write-back of agent output with `sourced-from` links and an `agent_id`.
7. **Provenance on every block and link.** This is the moat. Don't compromise on it.
8. **One reference agent**, picked for the strongest demand signal — likely **Customer Profile** for B2B SaaS (clear ROI: "your CRM + support + contracts in one queryable, cited record") *or* **Legal Case Files** if a design partner exists (higher willingness-to-pay, harder sales).

That is the entire MVP. Roughly 3–4 engineers, 4–6 months.

### What gets cut

| Feature | Cut because |
|---|---|
| AI-inferred typed links (contradicts/overrides/elaborates/etc.) | Quality and cost both unproven; can be added in v1 once link evaluation methodology exists |
| Synthesis Quorum + cryptographic attestation | Determinism premise is wrong; multi-signature pattern is overkill for v0 customers |
| Edge embeddings | Unspecified and speculative |
| Background grooming + fitness functions + adversarial pairing + tournament replacement + confidence decay + bounded budgets + source-block audits | Entire research program; not needed if synthesis is event-triggered, not continuous |
| Human-in-the-loop Bayesian sampling audit | Phase 3+ at earliest; v0 customers do not have enough scale for sampling to matter |
| External source ingestion + credibility weighting + Credibility Agent | Adds attack surface (untrusted ingest), legal-discovery complications, and a research problem (credibility scoring). Customers' internal docs are enough for v0 |
| Block-identity-shared-across-versions | Use document-level versioning until customers ask for finer granularity |
| Multiple reference agents (Handbook + Strategic + Quorum Panel + Credibility) | Pick one. Concretely build that one. Document it as the canonical example |
| Principal-Model unification (humans-as-agents-as-entities) | Cosmetic API elegance; ship a simple users table + API keys table with scopes |
| Future-LLM-I/O speculation in Flat File Ceiling | Distracting in a product spec. Keep the consistency-and-provenance argument; cut the futurism |
| "Vertically agnostic platform API" positioning | Pick one vertical for v0 and own it. Reposition as horizontal once one vertical is winning |

### What gets added

- **One concrete vertical positioning statement.** Pick legal, finance, or B2B-SaaS-customer-intelligence. The spec should read like a product for that buyer.
- **A pricing model.** Per-block-stored, per-synthesis, per-corpus — pick one and stress-test it against the unit economics.
- **A success metric.** "Customers measure X at 90 days." Without this, sales has no story.
- **A competitive differentiator paragraph.** Why Nexum and not Glean / Hebbia / LlamaIndex Cloud. The honest answer is probably "block-level provenance with typed-link-graph as a queryable platform, not a vertical app." That is fine — say it.
- **A security-and-compliance section.** Tenant isolation, encryption posture, audit logs, deletion semantics. Not a sales sheet — an architectural commitment.

## 4. Recommended next steps

1. **Cut the doc by half.** Remove Quorum, Grooming, HITL, Edge Embeddings, External Ingestion, AI-Inferred Typed Links, four of the five reference agents, and the future-LLM speculation. Keep what's left.
2. **Pick one vertical and one reference agent.** Rewrite the Initial Target Use Cases section as a single use case told concretely.
3. **Add the four missing sections:** pricing model, customer success metric, competitive differentiation, security/compliance.
4. **Pressure-test the unit economics** at the chosen pricing model against the inference + storage + parsing costs of a reference customer.
5. **Find one design partner** in the chosen vertical. Build for them. Resist generalizing until v0 ships and they renew.

The resulting product is smaller, more honest about what it does, and more likely to actually exist in twelve months than the current spec.

## Appendix: what's actually good

To balance the criticism — the parts of the current doc that should survive intact:

- The block-level addressability and provenance model is genuinely well-thought-through and is the durable value of the platform.
- The structural / semantic link distinction is right; the structural side ships immediately and is undervalued in similar products.
- Versioned, non-destructive, append-only data model is correct for the regulated-industry customer.
- The CLI-rebuttal paragraph in the Flat File Ceiling section is a strong, concrete framing.
- The decision to make synthesis a write-back of the same graph (not a separate "answers" table) is the right architectural call. It enables the audit story and shouldn't be diluted.

The product is in there. It just needs to stop trying to be five products at once.
